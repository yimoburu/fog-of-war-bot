# V4 research: imperfect-information search, belief quality and decision risk

Research checked November 13, 2026. This is a source review and experimental proposal, not a strength certificate. No third-party implementation or trained parameters were imported. Published and repository-reported measurements below were not reproduced here. Repository claims can be more recent than their README; the pinned executable source takes precedence when they conflict.

The immediate priority is to improve decisions among plausible moves and measure why they fail. A larger search or belief is useful only if it improves that decision within the complete turn budget. Neither the local results nor the external literature establishes a 2200–2500 MIT arena rating for this bot.

## What our evidence actually supports

[V3 validation](../outputs/validation-v3.md) reports 97 wins, one draw and 102 losses in the fresh 200-game comparison against frozen V2: 48.75%, insufficient for promotion. V2 remains released. A 1.66× search-only speedup did not establish stronger play. The primary run's 7,080 candidate responses averaged 166.73 ms, with a 180.825 ms 95th percentile and 202.79 ms maximum under the optional 180 ms budget. Host timing and the arena runtime differ.

The complete [root-coverage audit](../outputs/root-coverage-v3.json) gives a useful negative result. Hidden slider blockers caused the visible-safety filter to remove 34 truly safe moves on 23 of 7,080 turns, but never every safe move. None of the 102 terminal losing decisions lost a safe alternative through this filter. The 64-move cutoff applied once, in a winning game. At 82 terminal losses, immediately safe alternatives remained eligible after both filters; 20 positions had none. This does not show which alternatives would eventually win. It does identify the later shortlist, actual belief support and ranking as more promising diagnostic targets than rewriting those early filters.

The [two V2 loss reconstructions](v2-loss-review.md) suggest missing hidden promotions/rook branches and excessive sensitivity to one particle. Those particle banks were reconstructed, not logged during the original timed decisions. They are hypotheses about mechanisms, not exact recovered runtime state. V3's later failure also prevents treating a fix motivated by those two examples as a proven strength improvement.

The inspected implementation uses up to 64 retained positions, four worlds for deeper search, a twelve-move shortlist and depth-three determinization. Immediate-risk fractions and continuation values are heuristic. The common root action does not prevent future strategy fusion: deeper searches can implicitly make different choices in worlds the player could not distinguish. These observations refer to the V3 code inspected before V4 changes.

## Obscuro: the research architecture

Zhang and Sandholm's paper was submitted June 2, 2025 and revised February 28, 2026. Obscuro maintains observation-consistent positions, samples at most a few hundred roots, and carries its previous tree/strategy. KLUSS removes sufficiently remote knowledge relationships while unfreezing nearby strategies. Predictive CFR+ and one-sided growing-tree expansion alternate equilibrium updates and exploration; Stockfish 14 MultiPV depth-one evaluations supply bounded leaves. Resolve/Maxmargin gadgets and partial purification govern commitment. The Resolve distribution mixes uniform mass with prior-strategy reach; this is not a fitted opponent model. KLUSS lacks a universal safety guarantee, and last-iterate play is not the average-strategy convergence theorem.

Reported results include 834W/33D/133L against the previous agent and 16W/4L against the then top-rated Chess.com FoW player, rated 2318. The broader human sample was 97W/3L after 17 exclusions from 117 games. Human matches used a six-core desktop; AI experiments used five cores per side. Their usually five-second AI search allowance excludes position enumeration. These results are not MIT ratings or measurements at our 180 ms budget. [Paper and appendices](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01242v2), [version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01242)

No original author implementation was verified in this review. The two implementations below are independent adaptations; their deviations matter.

## Misty: useful failure reports, substantial integration work

Pinned source: [`brianhliou/misty`, `5ba2c31e52868ba79e34e3eb2e042e14847ab4a9`](https://github.com/brianhliou/misty/tree/5ba2c31e52868ba79e34e3eb2e042e14847ab4a9). The author's September 29 post, updated October 15, describes a Python/Rust implementation with 32 search worlds, 2,000 expanded leaves and a nominal five-second budget. Its 16-million-position cap and reported 7.7 GB peak exceed the MIT memory envelope. It reports 9–16-second decisions and a forfeit during belief growth. In profiling, the equilibrium pass consumed 69% of wall time; post-search guards also added latency. More worlds reduced iterations and depth at the same clock, so simply increasing the sample was not consistently beneficial. These are author measurements under their own conditions. [Implementation report](https://brianhliou.com/posts/misty-fog-of-war-engine/)

## opowell/obscuro-chess: weighting and fog-specific utility

Pinned source: [`opowell/obscuro-chess`, `008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f`](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/tree/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f). This JavaScript implementation uses a separate generic search package and Stockfish. The README retains older 246-game model descriptions and the parameter document retains an old sampling default. Those descriptions are stale relative to the code below.

**Belief model.** Current `movePrior.js` records fitting on 2,872 Chess.com fog games, 182,919 decisions and 1,604 players. It reports three-fold validation by game, move log-loss 3.002→2.661 nats and improved true-position belief log-loss. The learned conditional move prior includes a uniform mixture floor of 0.0391. These are probability-model results, not Elo. A uniform distribution over opponent moves is itself different from a uniform distribution over reachable positions, because parent branching and transpositions change mass. [`movePrior.js`](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/blob/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f/src/movePrior.js)

**Sampling and recovery.** Executable defaults are posterior-proportional sampling (`SAMPLE_ALPHA_DEFAULT=1`) and no additional posterior reach multiplier (`REACH_WEIGHTING_DEFAULT=0`). The population cap is one million, with systematic resampling; update guard is four seconds. When a sampled bank empties, up to three complete history replays attempt recovery. Each replayed update receives a fresh time guard, so total recovery can greatly exceed one move's allowance. Our implementation should preserve its shared absolute deadline rather than copy this policy. Recovery still triggers only after emptiness. [`exactBelief.js`](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/blob/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f/src/exactBelief.js)

**Utility.** The current eight-feature logistic model was fitted October 2 on 174,246 positions from 2,853 decisive fog games, using Stockfish evaluation and material features. Its generated source reports game-grouped three-fold log-loss of 0.5554 nats, versus 1.0040 for Stockfish's expected-score estimate. Draws were excluded, and this is prediction of human fog outcomes, not calibrated MIT self-play value. [`fogValueModel.js`](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/blob/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f/src/fogValueModel.js), [fitting script](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/blob/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f/scripts/fit-fog-value.mjs)

The repository reports that replacing full-information WDL utility with fog-fitted utility reduced king hangs from roughly 52–54% to 15% on 300 mined hidden-threat probes. A subsequent material-balance model reportedly scored 66.3%±4.3 over 60 seat-swapped pairs against its predecessor. Adding visibility features slightly improved prediction yet worsened threat probes and scored 46.7%±4.6 over 60 pairs. The author attributes this to observational visibility correlating with attack, rather than estimating the causal value of scouting. These measurements motivate our own controlled tests; the reported uncertainty and opponent/runtime are the author's, not ours. [`NEXT STEPS.md`](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/blob/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f/NEXT%20STEPS.md)

There is a concrete integration mismatch: its fog `getResult` checks captured kings and the 100-halfmove draw, but does not implement MIT's immediate exposure termination, threefold repetition or no-available-move loss there. Leaf king-exposure scoring does not make its match referee interchangeable. [`FogChess.js`](https://github.com/opowell/obscuro-chess/blob/008841f327a6a96c683652eca22586cca525946f/src/FogChess.js)

## Endgame theory and its limits

Gehnen and Stannat's January 21, 2026 paper proves winning strategies for king-and-queen and king-and-two-rooks against a lone king, but not universally for king-and-rook. The stated setup knows the initial opposing king position; subsequent moves are hidden. The analysis explicitly ignores draw rules. These are not guarantees under MIT's repetition and 100-halfmove termination. A future endgame module would need its own bounded-move proof or exhaustive check using our referee. [Original paper, rules and assumptions](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18813v1)

## POMCP and ISMCTS: what transfers

Silver and Veness's 2010 POMCP combines particle belief updates with Monte Carlo planning over action-observation history trees, requiring a generative model rather than explicit transition tables. Section 3.2 warns about deprivation with finite particle populations and describes practical reinvigoration using perturbed particles. Its optimal-policy result assumes the POMDP formulation and correct belief; it does not establish equilibrium against an unknown adaptive opponent. [Original NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2010/file/edfbe1afcf9246bb0d40eb4d8027d90f-Paper.pdf)

Our application inference: bounded rejuvenation is a defensible experiment, but new worlds must still pass the current official observation constraints. A repair that abandons historical consistency must say so. Calling a collection of plausible boards a posterior does not make it calibrated, and merely inserting synthetic particles cannot recover an unknown probability law.

Cowling, Powley and Whitehouse's 2012 ISMCTS paper explains why separate determinizations can choose incompatible future actions, and develops shared information-set trees. Its single-observer formulation assumes fully observable opponent actions; the multiple-observer formulation handles partially observed actions with player-specific trees. Reported benefits vary by domain, and additional branching can consume the gains. [Original author-hosted paper](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf)

Our application inference: FoW requires faithful observation-history keys for both players, since opponent moves are hidden. Sharing only the first move across minimax worlds does not implement ISMCTS. A small, correctly keyed prototype could be valuable later, but a full rewrite is a larger experiment than fixing the measured ranking/support bottleneck.

## Ranked experiments for our implementation

The following are our engineering inferences, not claims that the sources proved these changes will improve this bot.

| Priority | Experiment | Cost, risk and decision criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Record the actual decision path: current bank status/count/diversity, root risk estimates, twelve-move membership, searched worlds, completed depth and final ranking. Offline, classify failures as missing support, missing sample, shortlist exclusion or ranking error. | Small optional diagnostic overhead, but full banks enlarge logs. Logging must not change policy inputs or leak truth. A replay reconstructed later cannot recover timed sampling exactly. This resolves the main uncertainty left by the 82 eligible-safe terminal decisions. |
| 2 | Fit a compact immediate-king-loss predictor from local replay counterfactual actions, using observation/history features only; compare bank-only, model-only and bounded blends. | Existing V3 games can supply development labels. The target is immediate exposure, not eventual game value. A learned score must not certify safety, and correlation with the bank prevents treating the two as independent evidence. Advance only after held-out calibration and fresh match gains. |
| 3 | Optimize exact visibility matching, then spend saved work on representative belief/search coverage. Compare particle counts and root-world counts separately. | Differential testing must cover blocked pawn pushes, en passant, castling and every-ply observations. Extra worlds can reduce completed depth. Stratified threat sampling requires appropriate weights; an oversampled threat fraction is not a posterior probability. |
| 4 | Test a larger whole-turn budget, approximately 800–1500 ms, against V2 given the same budget. Keep a separate fixed-180-ms experiment to isolate algorithmic gains. | The official 180+2 clock permits more average work than our self-imposed 180 ms cap. It does not permit unbounded inference tails. Use one absolute deadline for observation processing, belief, recovery, scoring and search, retaining an output reserve. Report memory and high-percentile latency, not just means. |
| 5 | Add bounded rejuvenation or checkpointed history replay, with diversity diagnostics even while the bank remains nonempty. Preserve promotion and hidden-slider possibilities without inventing certainty. | Full-history consistency is stronger than current-view repair. Computation can be large; interrupt cleanly and mark approximate history. Compare support retention and selected-action risk on held-out replays before a fresh match test. |
| 6 | Improve continuation evaluation and information-set search after identifying the dominant remaining failure class. | A fog-specific result model needs representative labels; ordinary-chess WDL and visibility bonuses are not substitutes. A full CFR/KLUSS rewrite has greater implementation and validation cost than a focused ranking experiment. Search must share decisions across indistinguishable histories, including the opponent's information. |

For experiment 2, construct features from the exact observation prefix and known own moves available at that turn. Inferred material must account for possible hidden promotions; evaluator truth must never silently supply enemy inventory, last sightings or future observations. Counterfactual labels may use the official true board offline, then only fitted parameters and allowed features enter the policy. Group train/test splits by complete game and color-swapped seed pair, not action rows. Thousands of actions from one game are correlated. Report Brier/log-loss, calibration by risk band, top-choice immediate-loss rate, safe-alternative retention and complete match results. Overall classification accuracy is misleading because most legal actions survive immediately. Training on V3-versus-V2 positions describes that induced distribution, not all human or elite-arena opponents.

For experiment 4, a stronger result at a larger budget is a useful product result but not evidence that the algorithm itself improved. Preserve a compute-matched baseline and predeclare the comparison. More time does not make unsupported hypotheses true, nor fix a shortlisted move that is never searched.

The inspected search also stops at depth three or 18,000 nodes. Increasing milliseconds alone may therefore leave compute unused. Log the binding limit and completed depth before changing time, depth, node allowance or world count together.

## Options not justified for this iteration

- Widening the early safety filter or 64-move cap is low priority given the complete V3 audit; isolated anecdotes would overstate the measured impact.
- Importing an entire open engine is not a quick path to a verified MIT rating. Protocol/referee parity, offline dependencies, memory, timing and applicable licenses still need review. Neither independent implementation has a verified MIT rating in this research.
- Blindly replacing our evaluator with Stockfish voting or averaging retains strategy fusion. Replacing a decisive king-loss utility with ordinary-chess WDL can also favor resigning in positions that still have fog-specific chances.
- Exact enumeration without bounded computation is incompatible with robust clock handling. Conversely, hard-pruning unlikely histories without a recovery plan can permanently remove the true branch.
- A fitted information/visibility bonus is not evidence that scouting caused a better result. It needs controlled choices and full-game evaluation.
- Increasing model size, training a large policy from scratch or copying a paper's world count is not justified by a local development sample. Better calibration, speed or a saturated win rate against weak controls cannot substitute for an independent strength comparison.

The [earlier arena research](research-strength.md) records the MIT runtime, rating pool and submission caveats. Its dated leaderboard snapshot and early development results are historical context, not current strength evidence. The next candidate still needs fresh held-out games, unchanged source manifests, official-referee replay auditing and eventual Linux/arena measurement.
